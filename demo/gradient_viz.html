Understanding Gradient Flow

Healthy Gradients in Range ~0.001 – 10.0

Gradient norms are consistent across layers and epochs. The network is updating weights at an appropriate rate, enabling stable and effective learning.

Best practices to maintain:
  • Monitor gradient norms regularly to catch drift early
  • Keep learning rate moderate — too high risks exploding, too low slows learning
  • Use consistent weight initialization (He for ReLU, Xavier for tanh/sigmoid)
  • Add batch normalization in very deep networks as a preventive measure
Vanishing Gradients < 1e-5

Early layers receive negligible gradient signal. The network fails to learn deep representations, often manifesting as very slow training or stalled loss.

Fixes:
  • Use ReLU or Leaky ReLU activations (avoid sigmoid in deep nets)
  • Add batch normalization between layers
  • Use residual / skip connections
  • Apply careful weight initialization (He, Xavier)
Exploding Gradients > 100

Gradient norms are excessively large. Training is unstable: loss may diverge (NaN/Inf), and weight updates overshoot the optimum wildly.

Fixes:
  • Apply gradient clipping (clip norm to 1.0 or 5.0)
  • Lower the learning rate
  • Add weight regularization (L2 / weight decay)
  • Use batch normalization to stabilize activations
